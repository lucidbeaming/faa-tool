# 2026 FAA communication outage — facts

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FAA_communication_outage (read 2026-11-14).
The article is flagged as a current event; details may change.

## What happened

- November 13, 2026, 20:54 UTC (3:54 PM Eastern): the FAA issued a nationwide ground stop across the United States because of a communication outage.
- It started at Chicago's O'Hare and Midway airports, then the FAA ordered all airports to ground flights "due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety."
- It is only the third national ground stop in U.S. history. The other two: the September 11, 2001 attacks and the 2023 FAA system outage.

## Effects

- Departures from U.S. airports stopped. Domestic flights already in the air were allowed to continue to their destinations.
- International flights approaching the U.S. were handled case by case. International flights crossing U.S. airspace were affected too.
- Gates at big airports filled up; some arriving planes were diverted to smaller nearby airports.
- More than 10,000 flights were cancelled or delayed across the U.S.

## Not affected

- Flights within Alaska and within Hawaii appeared unaffected, as did non-mainland flights out of Hawaii.
- At 3 PM Pacific, Pacific-bound flights from San Francisco (to Hawaii and Asia) were cleared to depart.

## Cause

- A telecommunications problem affecting all of the Air Route Traffic Control Centers (the big "Center" facilities that tell pilots how to travel between airports while en route).
- Problems with radio frequencies were noticed in the hours before the ground stop.
- As of November 13, the exact cause was unclear.
- Reuters reported that the National Weather Service also had a nationwide radar outage and telecom problems around the same time. No confirmed link.

## Official reactions

- Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication of a "nefarious attack," and no connection to the 2026 Microsoft outage that began the day before.
- Nav Canada said all Canadian systems were working and could take diverted planes.
